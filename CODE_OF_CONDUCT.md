# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want taking part in Game Save to be a good experience for everybody, and
this project is small enough that how we treat each other is visible in every
commit.

We pledge to act in ways that make this project welcoming, thoughtful and
open:

- be kind and patient with people who are learning;
- be respectful of different opinions, experiences and levels of knowledge;
- give and accept constructive feedback gracefully;
- take responsibility for our mistakes, apologise to those affected, and learn
  from them;
- keep the focus on what is best for the project and for the people who use
  it.

## Our standards

**Examples of behaviour that helps:**

- saying what the problem is, and what a good solution would look like;
- reviewing the change, not the author;
- explaining the reasoning behind a decision;
- accepting that a maintainer may disagree, and that the decision is not a
  verdict on anybody;
- welcoming newcomers, including people who ask basic questions;
- saying "I do not know" and finding out together.

**Examples of behaviour that does not help:**

- personal attacks, insults, or anything that makes somebody feel unwelcome;
- dismissing a point because it is new, badly expressed, or from somebody
  who has not contributed before;
- publishing somebody's private information without permission;
- sending spam, or opening issues and pull requests only to advertise
  something else;
- pushing generated code that nobody has read or understood;
- sustained disruption of discussions.

## Scope

This applies to the issue tracker, the pull requests, the discussions, and
anywhere somebody is representing the project.

## Reporting

If you see something that does not belong, tell the maintainers privately.
They will look into it, and they will not repeat what you told them.

Reports are handled by the maintainers of the repository. They will keep the
identity of the reporter confidential.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying these standards and will take
fair and appropriate action in response to any behaviour they consider
unacceptable.

Depending on the severity, the response can be:

- a private word, and a change to the code or the wording;
- a warning, with no consequence if the behaviour stops;
- a temporary ban from any interaction with the project;
- a permanent ban from the project.
